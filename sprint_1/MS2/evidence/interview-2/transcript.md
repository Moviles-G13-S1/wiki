# Interview 2

*I’ll use JFS as an abbreviation for Juan Felipe Sáenz, the interviewer.*

**JFS:** Good afternoon. Today we are here with Catherine Jaque, a Systems and Computing Engineering student at Universidad de los Andes. We are going to ask her some questions about the application prototype we are planning. So, Cathe, tell me a little about yourself. What do you usually shop for online?

**Catherine:** Hi. I usually buy a lot of clothes, especially, as well as handbags and skincare products.

**JFS:** Okay, okay. How often would you say you browse online stores or social media looking for things you might want to buy?

**Catherine:** Hmm, I would say once or twice a week.

**JFS:** Okay, that’s fine. Tell me about the last time you saw something online that you wanted to buy but did not purchase right away, whether it was because of the price or something else. What did you do after you became interested in it?

**Catherine:** The day before yesterday, actually. I saw a handbag, but at that moment, I don’t know, I didn’t feel like transferring the money and going through the whole process. The only thing I did was save it. I think I’ll buy it when I feel like doing it and have the money.

**JFS:** And when you saw that handbag, for example, where did you save it? More generally, when you see something you want to buy but think, “I don’t want to buy it right now,” where do you keep it? Do you use a notes app or something similar? How and where do you save it?

**Catherine:** If it is on Instagram, I save it in my saved posts. But if it is on another website, I simply take a screenshot and try to find it later in my gallery, although sometimes it gets lost there.

**JFS:** Okay, that’s fine. Since you mentioned that things sometimes get lost, have you ever lost track of something you wanted to buy or forgotten about it completely? Maybe it was something you wanted in the moment but ultimately never bought. What happened?

**Catherine:** Yes. Yes, definitely. That mostly happens with products from websites where I simply take a screenshot. They end up being forgotten in my gallery.

**JFS:** When you are deciding between similar products, how do you usually compare them?

**Catherine:** Hmm, I think I look for different versions, perhaps using Google Lens or Pinterest. But when it comes to the exact same product, I don’t really compare it that much.

**JFS:** How often do you check whether the price of something you are interested in has increased or changed since the first time you saw it?

**Catherine:** Hmm, not very often. I think I only do that when the product is very expensive.

**JFS:** Okay, that’s fine. Have you ever shared your wishlist or shopping list with someone else, perhaps when thinking about a gift or a trip, or simply to ask for someone else’s opinion? How do you usually share it?

**Catherine:** Hmm, I have never shared the entire list. But when the topic comes up, I might say something like, “I saw this product on this website the other day, and I really loved it.” So, I might mention a particular product, but I do not share a complete list of products.

**JFS:** Okay. To start wrapping up, if you could organize everything you want to buy into separate lists, would that be useful to you? Why or why not?

**Catherine:** Yes, I think it would, especially if I were looking for, say, handbags in a particular color and also wanted to find the ones with the best prices. I think it would be especially useful for comparing prices.

**JFS:** Since you think that would be useful, what specific information about the items would you like to save? Would you want to save only the link, or also the price and image? What kind of information would you like to keep?

**Catherine:** Hmm, I think I would like it to show the website or place where the item is available and its price. I would also like the prices to be arranged from lowest to highest, as well as an image of the product so that I could compare the options.

**JFS:** This question is somewhat related to what you said earlier about whether you have ever shared your wishlist. Would you prefer the list you create to be private, or would you want anyone to be able to see it, like, “This is Cathe’s list and these are the things she is interested in”?

**Catherine:** I think I would like to be able to choose. In other words, I would not want my entire list to be private or my entire list to be public. I would like to be able to classify—

**JFS:** To select which items... Okay.

**Catherine:** Which items are public and which ones are private, because there may be situations in which I want to share certain items.

**JFS:** If you had to choose an app for creating wishlists, what would it need to offer for you to actually use an app like this?

**Catherine:** Hmm, first, I think it should be easy to access and have a visually appealing user interface. It should not contain too much information. It should mainly show my wishlist and perhaps one or two additional options.

**JFS:** Okay. Finally, is there anything about the way you organize the things you want to buy that you find frustrating and that we have not discussed during this interview?

**Catherine:** Hmm, I think it is the fact that everything is divided among different apps and websites. That is what frustrates me because there is no single place where I can save all the products I find.

**JFS:** Okay, that’s all. Thank you very much for your time.

**Catherine:** Sure.
